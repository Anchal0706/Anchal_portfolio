This is the architecture-defining call. Each option is genuinely different, not stylistic.

A · Linear-then-chat

Cinematic film, chat at the end

Visitor scrolls through the 6-chapter cinematic film of her career first. At the end of chapter 6, the chat reveals as a finale: "Now, ask her anything."

  • Pro: cleanest narrative; awards-bait; chat feels earned
  • Pro: simplest to build — chat is one component, not a layer
  • Con: impatient visitors may bounce before reaching chat
  • Con: chat is "feature," not the headline

B · Chat-first

Chat is the headline; film is "expand"

Land in the WebGPU semantic field. The first thing you see is the prompt. Below: "or take the cinematic tour" — clicking enters the linear film. Two doors, chat is the front one.

  • Pro: chat is unmistakably THE thing — strongest brand bet
  • Pro: recruiters/strangers can interrogate the portfolio in 30 seconds
  • Con: film becomes a side-attraction (less SOTY-eligible)
  • Con: empty chat box is intimidating; needs strong starter prompts
ask while you scroll · "what was her SEO at qorvatech?"
C · HUD overlay

Film + chat at all times

The 3D film plays in the background; the chat is always present as a subtle HUD bar at the bottom. Asking a question pauses the film and pulls you to the relevant chapter. Both modes coexist.

  • Pro: most feature-dense; user can choose their mode any time
  • Pro: answers can literally cite the chapter you're seeing
  • Con: highest complexity; coupling film state ↔ chat state is gnarly
  • Con: two interaction systems competing for attention; risk: feels busy

D · Chapter-by-chapter

Each chapter ends with a contextual prompt

Same cinematic film as A, but each chapter ends with a chat moment scoped to that chapter: "ask about her RLHF work." Six small grounded chats, not one big open-ended one. RAG corpus filters per chapter.

  • Pro: chat questions are guided → much higher answer quality
  • Pro: rhythmic — film, chat, film, chat — keeps engagement
  • Pro: RAG is easier (smaller corpus per chapter)
  • Con: no global "ask anything" entry — power users may want it
My recommendation: D (Chapter-by-chapter) with a persistent "ask anything" button in the corner that escapes to a global chat (the B mode). That's the only option that serves both the cinematic-film judges and the practical visitor. C looks tempting but is a trap — film-state + chat-state coupling will eat 2 weeks of bug-fixing.
